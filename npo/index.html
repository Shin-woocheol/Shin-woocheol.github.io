For few-step generative model alignment, direct fine-tuning can suffer from reward over‑optimization, while test-time optimization incurs substantial inference cost. NPO efficiently aligns few-step generative models using only black‑box rewards, while maintaining high sample quality and fast inference.

Abstract

Few-step generative models produce high-quality samples in one or a few evaluations. However, steering them toward desired properties remains difficult, especially when only black-box reward evaluations are available. While one could apply approaches developed for iterative diffusion and flow-based models, such as direct RL fine-tuning or test-time scaling, we find that neither transfers well to the few-step regime: direct fine-tuning suffers from severe reward over-optimization, and test-time scaling sacrifices the efficiency advantage of few-step generation.

We propose Noise Policy Optimization (NPO), which explicitly targets the alignment problem for few-step generative models. The key idea is to learn an amortized flow policy over the initial noise space of the generator. The trained policy transports Gaussian noise to a reward-tilted noise distribution, whose pushforward distribution through the frozen generator is exactly the reward-tilted output distribution. By keeping the generator frozen, we can effectively utilize the knowledge encoded in the pretrained model, yielding a better reward-quality trade-off than direct fine-tuning. The amortized policy moves the search cost into training, so inference adds only a lightweight noise sampling step on top of the pretrained model. To improve sample efficiency during training, we use ELBO-based likelihood estimation that requires only sampled endpoints rather than simulated trajectories.

Across image, language, and protein structure generation, NPO consistently achieves a better reward-quality trade-off than direct fine-tuning, and matches test-time search methods with 16–549× lower inference cost.

Method

Noise-space target

Rather than modifying the generator \(g_\theta\), NPO changes its input distribution while keeping the generator fixed. Sampling from the reward-tilted output distribution \(p^\star\) can be reduced to sampling from the reward-tilted noise distribution \[ q^\star(z)\propto q_{\mathrm{base}}(z)\exp\!\left(\frac{r(g_\theta(z))}{\alpha}\right) \] whose pushforward through \(g_\theta\) is exactly \(p^\star\), i.e., \(g_{\theta\#}q^\star=p^\star\).

Amortized flow policy

We learn a flow policy \(q_\psi\) to approximate the reward-tilted noise distribution \(q^\star\) by minimizing the reverse KL divergence \[ \min_\psi D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\psi\|q^\star), \] which is equivalent to maximizing reward while regularizing \(q_\psi\) toward the initial noise distribution \(q_{\mathrm{base}}\). By amortizing the noise transformation, NPO moves the cost of test-time search to training.

ELBO-based policy optimization

Policy-gradient optimization requires estimating the endpoint likelihood \[ \log q_\psi(z_0). \] We use an ELBO-based estimator that operates on sampled endpoints and forward-noising pairs \((t,\epsilon)\), rather than conventional trajectory-based likelihood estimation. This avoids dependence on simulated rollout trajectories, providing a more efficient signal for policy updates.

Results

We evaluate NPO across image, language, and protein structure generation, focusing on three questions:

  1. Reward–quality trade-off. Does NPO achieve a better reward–quality trade-off than direct generator fine-tuning?
  2. Inference efficiency. Does NPO achieve competitive rewards at lower inference cost than test-time optimization?
  3. Training efficiency. Does NPO reach comparable rewards with fewer reward calls than trajectory-based noise policies?

Setup. We use SANA-Sprint and SD-Turbo with one- and four-step sampling as pretrained models. Target rewards: LAION aesthetic score and JPEG compressibility, optimized without reward gradients. JPEG compressibility comes from a non-differentiable encoder, and we treat the aesthetic predictor as a black box as well, so neither reward gradient is used. We use ImageReward and LPIPS diversity to assess sample quality and diversity, respectively.

Under one-step sampling, NPO improves both target rewards on both generators, whereas Flow-GRPO and FAV-B show limited or no improvement. Under four-step sampling, Flow-GRPO raises the target reward but loses ImageReward and diversity, while NPO preserves both.

Setup. We use a five-step distilled protein backbone generator as the pretrained model. The target reward, secondary-structure (SS) composition, favors diverse compositions with extra weight on \(\beta\)-sheets. Because the reward relies on categorical assignments made after generation, its gradient is not available. Designability measures whether generated backbones can be self-consistently realized by designed amino-acid sequences, serving as a measure of plausibility. Pairwise TM-score measures structural diversity.

1Reward–quality trade-off

Direct fine-tuning yields limited reward improvements while sharply degrading designability. In particular, Flow-GRPO reduces designability to zero, meaning that none of the evaluated backbones are designable. In contrast, NPO substantially improves the reward while retaining much higher designability.
